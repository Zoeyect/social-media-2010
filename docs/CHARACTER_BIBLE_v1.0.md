# SOCIAL MEDIA, 2010 — Character Bible v1.0

**Status:** Canonical  
**Setting:** Los Angeles, October 2010  
**Core function:** These are not nine NPCs for the user to observe. They are nine believable people who seem to have existed somewhere in the user's own 2010 social world.

The user occupies two positions simultaneously:

- **Observer:** scrolling through fragments of other people's lives.
- **Participant:** receiving messages, comments, invitations, mentions, likes, jokes, and small signs of attention.

The experience should create the feeling:

> “I knew someone like this.”

or, occasionally:

> “I think I would have had a crush on this person.”

But the system **never canonically decides who the user loved, dated, went to school with, worked with, or was closest to.**

The emotional target is not dramatic nostalgia. It is **recognition**: old Facebook photos, awkward status updates, blurry phone cameras, inside jokes you only half understand, somebody staying online too late, somebody you used to notice at school, somebody whose name appearing on your screen made you slightly happier.

---

# 0. Global Character Rules

## User relationship rule

Every character knows the user well enough for interaction to feel plausible.

However, never establish:

- exact friendship duration
- exact school class
- romantic history
- whether the user is male/female
- whether the user is a student or worker
- whether the user plays sports
- whether the user attends the same school
- whether the user shares the character's interests
- whether the user has dated any character

Use ambiguous interaction such as:

- `you coming?`
- `where are you`
- `lol did you see this`
- `thought you'd like this`
- `hey`
- tagging the user in a photo
- replying to something the user did
- sending a link without explanation

Avoid:

- `remember when we started freshman year together`
- `best friends since 7th grade`
- `your basketball practice`
- `your office`
- `your boyfriend/girlfriend`
- `you always love this band`

# 1. Katie

## Identity

**Name:** Katie  
**Age:** 14  
**Ethnicity:** Not canonically specified  
**Location:** Los Angeles area  
**Life stage:** Middle/high-school-age teenager  
**Core archetype:** **Everyday closeness**

Katie should feel less like a “character” and more like somebody who simply happens to be in the user's life.

## Family

- Older brother: **Ben**, 23
- Lives at home with family.
- Family itself should remain mostly ordinary and underwritten.
- Ben can naturally appear in household photos or comments.

Katie and Ben's sibling relationship is canonical.

## School / occupation

Student.

Do not specify a prestigious school, special program, social status, or strong extracurricular identity.

She is deliberately ordinary.

## Personality

- warm
- familiar
- mildly impulsive
- expressive without being theatrical
- sometimes bored
- sometimes mildly dramatic in the completely normal teenage sense
- comfortable enough around the user that she does not need to impress them

Katie's defining trait is **low social performance**.

She posts because something happened, not because she is building an image.

## Speech

Short, casual, frequently lowercase.

Typical vocabulary:

- `lol`
- `omg`
- `ugh`
- `wait`
- `seriously`
- `hahaha`
- `where are you`
- `call me`
- `come over`

Occasional repeated letters are appropriate:

- `noooo`
- `soooo bored`

Do not make every sentence exaggerated.

### Example energy

`mom bought the wrong cereal again`

`where are you??`

`lol ben is being annoying`

## Facebook

**High importance.**

Katie is a natural Facebook user.

Typical activity:

- status updates about mundane things
- wall posts
- comments between friends
- tagged family photos
- school photos
- random albums
- casual Likes
- conversations that make sense only to people involved

Her albums should feel poorly curated.

Possible album names:

- `random`
- `summer :)`
- `school`
- `lol`
- automatic-looking date/event names

## Twitter

Low or nonexistent.

If she has Twitter, she barely uses it.

Facebook should remain her natural home.

## Instagram

Probably **not an early adopter** in October 2010.

Do not put Katie on Instagram simply because the app exists.

## Foursquare

Rare.

Could appear once through friends, but should not become part of her identity.

## Social graph

Strongest canonical relationship:

**Katie ↔ Ben:** siblings.

She can plausibly know June, Jack, Chris, etc., but these relationships should emerge incidentally rather than through explicit exposition.

## User emotional role

Katie represents:

**the person whose presence makes the phone feel like home.**

She is not primarily a crush archetype.

Her interactions make the user feel included in an existing everyday life.

## Never write Katie as

- social-media influencer prototype
- fashion girl
- unusually witty internet personality
- precocious adult
- sexually provocative
- melodramatic teen-TV character
- obsessive romantic interest
- somebody whose entire identity is “cute”

## Easter eggs

- Ben occasionally comments on her posts.
- Katie complains about Ben in harmless sibling ways.
- Ben may appear unintentionally in the background of her photos.
- Her wall can contain tiny references to events visible elsewhere in the experience.

# 2. Matteo “Matt” Ricci

## Identity

**Full name:** Matteo Lee Ricci  
**Everyday name:** Matt Ricci  
**Age:** approximately 17–18  
**Height:** approximately 180 cm  
**Ethnicity:** Asian / Italian mixed heritage  
**Core archetype:** **Quiet computer guy**

His historical-name coincidence with Matteo Ricci / 利玛窦 is canonical.

## Family

Family details remain largely unspecified.

His Italian and Asian heritage should be visible naturally through name, appearance, occasional family references, or jokes—not exposition dumps.

Do not make ethnicity the core of his personality.

## School / occupation

Student.

Strong interest in:

- computers
- coding
- bugs
- software
- hardware
- late-night troubleshooting
- tech news

He is not a startup founder and should not sound like a 2020s Silicon Valley engineer.

## Personality

- introverted
- observant
- dry
- smart
- occasionally savage with people he knows well
- not socially helpless
- doesn't volunteer much personal information
- more expressive online than in person

Matt's attraction comes partly from **understatement**.

He should rarely act as though he knows he is attractive.

## Speech

Minimal.

Deadpan humor.

Examples of rhythm:

`fixed it`

`never mind`

`that made it worse`

`why is this still awake`

`3 hours for one missing semicolon`

He usually avoids:

- excessive emoticons
- long emotional Facebook paragraphs
- performative sarcasm
- constant `lol`

One quiet sentence can be enough.

## Facebook

Moderate use.

Facebook Matt is more personal than Twitter Matt.

Possible content:

- tagged group photos
- friends writing on his Wall
- occasional school event
- tech-related status
- friends teasing him

He doesn't upload dozens of selfies.

## Twitter

**Very high importance.**

Matt is one of the characters who makes 2010 Twitter feel alive.

Typical subjects:

- code
- bugs
- computers
- browsers
- Apple / Microsoft / Google
- late nights
- weird internet observations
- links
- terse replies

Tweets can happen at strange hours.

His Twitter should make the user realize:

> Matt apparently talks a lot more when nobody is physically standing in front of him.

## Instagram

Not central.

He may know what it is because he follows technology, but avoid making him a heavy user.

A subtle early-adopter reference is enough.

## Foursquare

Low use.

Could occasionally check into:

- coffee shop
- electronics store
- campus
- restaurant

but shouldn't care about Mayor status.

## Social graph

Matt can plausibly overlap with the younger school/social group without becoming part of Jack's crowd.

He is likely known *by* more people than he actively socializes with.

## User emotional role

Matt represents:

**the person who is quiet in real life but whose online presence makes you curious about him.**

Potential nostalgic crush energy is allowed, but never canonically romantic.

Small interactions work better than flirting.

For example:

Matt sends the user a strange link at 1:08 AM.

No explanation.

That is more effective than:

`hey beautiful ;)`

## Never write Matt as

- stereotypical antisocial genius
- hacker cliché
- socially incompetent
- tech CEO prototype
- hyper-confident “nerd hot guy”
- nonstop meme machine
- romantic poet
- modern Discord/Reddit programmer transplanted into 2010

## Easter eggs

Friends occasionally tease him about **Matteo Ricci / 利玛窦**.

Matt's response should be dry rather than explanatory.

Example energy:

`yes. china was great in 1601.`

Recurring tiny computer problems can appear across days.

One bug he complains about on Twitter can quietly be “fixed” later without explanation.

## Twitter

Low-to-moderate.

No carefully curated voice.

## Instagram

Possible but not important.

Moderate.

- `era`
- `core`

If he uses it, content should skew toward:

- records

Moderate-low.

**the person you partially remember through songs.**

# 7. Jack

## Identity

**Name:** Jack  
**Age:** 18
**Height:** approximately 190 cm  
**Ethnicity / ancestry:** white American with Germanic ancestry  
**Core archetype:** **Popular football captain**

Appearance continuity:

- blond
- tall
- athletic / muscular
- open, handsome smile
- physically confident
- unmistakably high-school-age despite athletic build

## Family

Not central.

Avoid wealthy-TV-family exposition unless later explicitly established.

His social status comes from school life, not family privilege.

## School / occupation

High-school student.

Football team captain.

**School team only.**

Never present him as:

- professional
- college athlete
- adult league player

Any uniforms must look like plausible high-school football uniforms.

Avoid invented team branding that accidentally reads as professional or institutionally specific unless intentionally designed.

## Personality

- confident
- upbeat
- socially fluent
- friendly
- comfortable around attention
- competitive
- easy laugh
- remembers people's names
- not especially introspective online

Important:

Jack should **not** be an asshole.

The more interesting nostalgic version is the popular guy who was actually nice enough that liking him felt even more dangerous.

## Speech

Simple, energetic.

Examples:

`great game tonight`

`thanks everyone`

`who's coming`

`haha dude`

`see you guys later`

`good luck tomorrow`

Occasional caps are fine but not every post.

## Facebook

**Extremely high importance.**

Facebook is where Jack's social status becomes visible.

Signals:

- many tagged photos
- many comments
- wall posts from different kinds of people
- birthday messages
- sports photos
- group photos
- invitations
- party references
- casual interactions across several social circles

Do not tell users:

> Jack is popular.

Make them **see it structurally**.

## Twitter

Moderate-low.

Could exist, but Facebook is much more revealing.

Tweets mostly:

- game
- friends
- quick jokes
- plans

He shouldn't suddenly become a thoughtful Twitter essayist.

## Instagram

Possible but not core.

He may join because other people join.

He would probably post very ordinary things.

## Foursquare

Moderate.

Useful for:

- food
- mall
- game-related location
- social hangouts

He does not need to optimize points.

## Social graph

Jack has the **widest social graph**.

He should plausibly touch multiple characters without becoming everybody's best friend.

Potential overlap:

- June through school/social life
- Chris through sports/social circles
- Katie through broader school/friend network
- others through parties or mutual friends

The famous “Jack party” should work as a **social intersection**, not a forced story chapter.

## User emotional role

Jack carries one of the strongest **school-crush memory archetypes**.

Not because he flirts aggressively.

Because:

- he notices the user
- he answers
- he invites broadly
- his name appears everywhere
- lots of other people seem to know him
- occasionally a direct interaction feels disproportionately important

For example:

`you coming tonight?`

is stronger than an explicit confession.

The user should be allowed to think:

> “Did he send that to everyone?”

That ambiguity is the point.

## Never write Jack as

- bully
- arrogant quarterback stereotype
- sex-obsessed teenager
- womanizer
- rich-kid villain
- adult-looking professional athlete
- emotionally tortured heartthrob
- main character of the entire project

## Easter eggs

- Jack appears in many unrelated people's tagged photos.
- Someone comments before Jack even uploads anything himself.
- Party content from several characters may triangulate the same evening.
- A mundane one-to-one message from Jack can feel much more emotionally significant than its actual wording.

# 12. Crush Design Rule

The project may evoke old crushes, but **no character is the canonical love interest**.

Potential crush energy should be generated through micro-signals:

- seeing someone's name repeatedly
- somebody unexpectedly replying
- being tagged
- a private message arriving late at night
- a friend uploading a particularly good photo of someone
- `you coming?`
- receiving a song
- seeing someone online
- noticing who comments on whose Wall
- discovering that somebody attended the same event
- somebody remembering a minor detail
- a profile picture changing

Avoid explicit romance systems such as:

- affection meters
- route selection
- dating flags
- canonical confession
- jealousy plot
- love triangles
- “correct” romantic choice

The user's own memory should perform that interpretation.

---

# 13. Nostalgia Rule

Nostalgia must come primarily from **banality preserved by technology**.

The strongest material is often not:

> “The best night of our lives.”

It is:

> `at target lol`

> `call me`

> `uploaded 37 new photos`

> `can't sleep`

> `new phone :)`

> `who wants food`

> somebody becoming Mayor of a Starbucks

> a badly exposed football photo

> a song embedded from YouTube

> an album called `randommm`

> an accidental blurry picture

> a Wall conversation nobody else understands

> a person who hasn't posted in three days suddenly appearing online

The project succeeds when users realize that the things that once felt disposable are now the things that feel most emotionally specific.

# 15. Final Canon

The nine characters should never compete to become the protagonist.

The protagonist is the person holding the phone.

Each character supplies a different form of memory:

**Katie — familiarity**  
**Matt — curiosity**  
**Alex — ordinariness**  
**Chris — participation**  
**Jay — association**  
**June — visual memory**  
**Jack — social gravity**  
**Ben — growing up**  
**Luca — late-night life**

Together they should create a social network in which the user can simultaneously:

**watch other people's lives, feel included in them, and project their own forgotten relationships into the gaps.**

The gaps are not missing writing.

**The gaps are the interaction design.**
