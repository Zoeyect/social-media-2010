# Core Social Character Bible v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This document is the canonical fictional social-character reference for **SOCIAL MEDIA, 2010**. It locks nine persistent identities whose repeated names, habits, relationships, locations, messages, and posting patterns make the phone feel owned by someone with a recognizable social circle.

The characters do not introduce or explain themselves in runtime content. Identity should emerge through fragments across Twitter, Facebook, Flickr, Foursquare, Tumblr, Instagram, and private-message surfaces. This is a consistency guide, not a complete biography or a quota requiring every character in every app.

Exactly these nine characters are canonical: Katie, Matt, Alex, Chris, Jay, June, Jack, Ben, and Luca. A new named filler character requires explicit narrative justification.

## 2. Character system principles

- Same canonical ID always means the same person across apps.
- Static identity metadata never stores mutable follow, friend, unread, reply, like, or comment state.
- Repetition should reveal character indirectly; runtime copy should avoid biography-like exposition.
- Character, app, hour, 2010 vernacular, and narrative restraint must all fit before content is added.
- The circle includes young social contacts and working adults; it must not read as an all-student cast.
- No romantic relationship between core characters is canonical unless separately approved.
- All identities are fictional. Real-world existence must never be implied.

Data categories:

| Category | Characters | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `core-friend` | Katie, Matt, Alex, Chris, Jay | Recurring ordinary contacts across social surfaces |
| `narrative-contact` | June, Jack | Characters with existing scheduled Facebook significance |
| `extended-friend` | Ben, Luca | Working-adult anchors connected to the broader circle |

## 14. Language and content consistency

Every proposed fictional item must pass five checks:

1. Character fit: the subject and phrasing plausibly belong to this person.
2. App fit: the content suits the specific 2010 platform and surface.
3. Time fit: the action and tone make sense at the simulated hour.
4. Vernacular fit: Twitter copy follows the Early Twitter Vernacular Reference Corpus where applicable.
5. Narrative restraint: the item reveals a fragment instead of explaining the character.

Tone examples in this Bible are guidance only. They should not be copied into runtime merely to demonstrate a trait. Repetition, occasional cross-character context and mundane details should do the characterization work.

## User Projection Boundary

The canonical characters may be specific; the session owner must remain deliberately under-specified. The internal design principle is: "This is your 2010 phone. These are the kinds of friends you might have."

Do not canonically assign the user a hobby, occupation, student status, fixed personality, romantic interest, party preference, or personal history. Friends may express their own interests and circumstances, while prompts to the user should remain open and user responses should be explicitly chosen.

Accepting, replying, following, liking, or dismissing establishes only the action the player took. It must not silently establish deeper friendship, enthusiasm, attendance, identity, or preference. See `docs/design/user-projection-social-circle-principle-v0.1.md` for the project-level rules and content audit.
